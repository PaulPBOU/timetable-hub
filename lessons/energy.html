WHY SAVE ENERGY?

Homes use energy for lighting, heating, cooling and . Saving energy means using less to do everyday jobs.

Many power stations burn fossil fuels, such as coal and gas. This releases greenhouse gases that warm the planet. Using less energy can reduce these emissions.

SMALL CHANGES ADD UP

Simple daily habits can reduce wasted energy and lower household bills.

LIGHTS & DEVICES

💡 Electricity & Standby Power

Lights and electronic devices use electricity. Some devices still draw power on , even when their screens are dark.

SIMPLE WAYS TO SAVE

  • ✓ Turn off lights when you leave a room.
  • ✓ Use natural daylight when possible.
  • ✓ Ask an adult to switch unused devices off at the wall.
  • ! Keep fridges, freezers and essential medical equipment switched on.

A BRIGHTER CHOICE

An LED light bulb uses less electricity than an old incandescent bulb.

Room Guide 02

HEATING & COOLING

🌡️ Indoor Comfort & Heat Transfer

Heaters and air conditioners use energy to change indoor temperatures. Thick, well-fitted curtains help slow heat transfer through windows.

KEEP COMFORTABLE

  • ☀️ In summer, close blinds during the hottest part of the day.
  • ❄️ In winter, let sunshine in during the day.
  • ✓ Close doors to rooms that are not being heated or cooled.
Room Guide 03

WATER & WASHING

💧 Hot Water & Laundry

Heating water uses energy. Shorter showers use less hot water, so less energy is needed to heat it.

WASH WISELY

  • ✓ Keep showers short.
  • ✓ Ask an adult to choose a cold wash when suitable.
  • ✓ Wait for a full load before running the washing machine.

LET CLOTHES DRY NATURALLY

A sunny, breezy clothesline dries wet clothes without using an electric tumble dryer.

Word Check

Evaporate means to change from liquid water into water vapour.
